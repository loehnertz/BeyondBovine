# 2026-09-25: Fed-batch prediction with the step 5 cell model

**Status:** exploratory increment, agreed with Jakob. Nothing was fitted to the fed-batch data. It is a pure test.

## What was built

- **`explorations/007_fed_batch.py`:** the cell model from 005 with its calibrated parameters unchanged. The operation follows Moreno-Paz et al. 2022:
  - a batch on 2.5 g/kg glucose
  - an exponential feed from 4 h, designed for μ = 0.05 1/h
  - a feed of 209 g/kg glucose plus 7.67 g/kg ethanol
  - volume growth
- **Oxygen uptake and CO₂ output** come from carbon and degree-of-reduction balances of each route: respiration, fermentation, and ethanol respiration.
  - **Verification:** the derived 2.31 O₂ and 2.48 CO₂ per respired glucose match the van Hoek chemostat (about 2.3 and 2.5) without fitting.
- **Deliberately not modelled:** an oxygen-supply limit.
- **Assumptions:**
  - The unreported initial feed rate follows the standard design rule. It assumes full respiratory conversion of the batch glucose, giving F0 = 0.24 g/h.
  - The unreported inoculum is shown as a range, 0.02–0.1 g/kg, starting either repressed or derepressed.
- **Data:** `explorations/data/morenopaz2022_fedbatch.csv`, copied unchanged (MIT licence).

## Results

- **20–97 h, the exponential feed phase:** the predicted OUR and CPR follow the data closely.
  - 45 h: OUR 13.2 vs 12.7.
  - 98 h: OUR 87.6 vs 84.7, CPR 88.9 vs 86.2.
  - This is the regime argued to resemble the chemostat, with adapted cells at low sugar. The prediction supports that reframing, for a third strain, CEN.PK113-7D.
- **0–15 h, batch phase and feed start:** roughly right. The OUR peak near 10 h is reproduced (11.9 vs 13.9). The data's dip at feed start is deeper (0.9 vs 3.2 at 15 h).
- **After about 97 h:**
  - The data jump abruptly to about 140–150 mmol/kg/h. The data also have a gap from about 81 to 97 h.
  - OUR then falls while CPR stays higher, and RQ rises to about 1.3–1.4.
  - The model keeps rising at RQ ≈ 1.0.
  - This is the predicted miss: fermentation while oxygen uptake falls, consistent with an oxygen-supply limit. However, the abrupt step is not physiological, because cells cannot raise uptake by 50 % instantly. It suggests an undocumented process change, such as feed, aeration or pressure. The late phase therefore cannot be attributed cleanly to oxygen limitation.
- **RQ above 1 at 15–60 h, falling from about 1.6 to 1.05 as the rates grow:** this shape is typical of a small constant offset in the gas analysis, about 2 mmol/kg/h in CPR. The measured OUR at t = 0 is also about −1. This is probably not physiology; unverified.
- **Biomass:**
  - The model is about 30 % below the data at 20–70 h (for example 25.6 vs 36.7 g/kg at 70 h), and it ends close (80.5 vs 82.2 at 118 h).
  - Since the gas fluxes match, the biomass gap points to either the biomass measurement, a different yield for CEN.PK, or the feed assumption.
  - Not resolved. There are only 5 points.

## What was learned

- In the adapted, low-sugar fed-batch regime, the step 5 model predicts gas exchange well for a strain it was never calibrated on.
- The late phase shows the predicted signature of fermentation with falling oxygen uptake, but a process change confounds it.
- Oxygen limitation is the natural next mechanism, and it links back to the original fed-batch question. It still needs a clean dataset or a sourced oxygen-transfer capacity. **Setting the ceiling from this same late phase would be calibration, not a test.**

## Addendum: the late phase is not a clean oxygen ceiling (checked before building an oxygen limit)

Medians from the data (OUR and CPR in mmol/kg/h):

| Window | OUR | CPR | RQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 97–99 h | 80 | 82 | 1.02 |
| 99–103 h | 130 | 130 | 1.00 |
| **103–108 h** | **153** | **150** | **0.98** |
| 108–112 h | 124 | 146 | 1.18 |
| 112–118 h | 97 | 128 | 1.31 |
| 118–123 h | 80 | 109 | 1.36 |

- **The vessel was able to supply about 150 mmol O₂/kg/h** during a fully respiratory interval (103–108 h, RQ ≈ 1).
- **Oxygen uptake then fell to about 80, far below that demonstrated capacity,** while RQ rose.
- **A fixed oxygen ceiling cannot produce this.** At a ceiling, uptake would plateau near the capacity, not halve.
- **Candidate explanations, both requiring undocumented operating information:**
  1. The aeration or stirring was changed near the end of the run.
  2. A feed increase near 99 h, suggested by the step, pushed uptake above the overflow threshold. Glucose then accumulated, and repression gradually lowered respiratory capacity. In that case OUR would fall and RQ would rise, which is the long-term Crabtree mechanism already in the model.
- **Consequence:** this dataset cannot test an oxygen-supply limit. Asking the authors for the feed and aeration logs would settle it.
